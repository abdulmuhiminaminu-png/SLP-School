# Level 6 — Applying SLP Live

## What this level covers
Taking the framework from Level 5 and applying it to real, live-looking charts —
inducement, confirmation entries, the tools used to mark setups, and checking
market bias.

## Inducement: waiting for the trap
After the Break of Structure, price should not go straight back to the order
block. A valid setup needs inducement — a pullback that comes close to the zone
and traps early buyers or sellers who jumped in too soon, before the real move
happens. Without inducement, a setup is weaker and more likely to be a trap
itself.

## Confirmation entry: rejection inside the zone
Confirmation entry comes from a rejection inside the zone — price tapping into
the order block and clearly rejecting away from it, not just touching it and
continuing through. Hakimi FX personally uses limit orders placed inside the
zone to grab this liquidity directly, rather than waiting to manually confirm
and enter market.

## Tools used to mark a setup
- Rectangle: marks the order block / POI zone on the chart.
- Trend line: confirms that the inducement pullback comes close enough to the
  zone to count.

## Market bias correlation
A setup must correlate with market bias — the broader direction the market is
already leaning on a higher timeframe. An order block that looks perfect but
goes against the higher-timeframe bias is a much weaker trade than one that
agrees with it. Always check bias before taking a setup, not after.

## Engineered liquidity
An additional confirmation tool: a sweep of a nearby level that still does NOT
enter the order block zone itself. This shows the market grabbing liquidity
just outside the zone — a sign that smart money is active around that area,
adding extra confidence to the setup without needing price to actually trade
into the zone yet.

## Common mistakes at this level
- Entering the moment price touches the zone, without waiting for a genuine
  rejection.
- Ignoring market bias and taking a setup purely because the order block
  "looks" clean.
- Confusing engineered liquidity (a sweep that stays outside the zone) with
  the actual entry trigger (rejection inside the zone) — they are two
  different, separate confirmations.

## What a student must demonstrate to pass this level
Given a chart showing an order block, identify whether inducement occurred,
locate a valid rejection entry, check whether the setup matches the
higher-timeframe bias, and note whether any engineered liquidity is visible
nearby.
