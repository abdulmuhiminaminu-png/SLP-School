# Level 1 — What Moves Price

## What this level covers
The absolute foundation: what price actually is, and why it moves.

## The core idea
Price moves because of an imbalance between buyers and sellers. When more
traders want to buy than sell at a given price, price rises to find sellers
willing to sell higher. When more want to sell than buy, price falls to
find buyers willing to buy lower. Every candle on a chart is really just a
record of that tug-of-war over a fixed period of time.

## Key concepts to teach
- **Support and resistance**: price levels where buying or selling pressure
  has historically been strong enough to pause or reverse a move.
- **Highs and lows**: a chart is really just a sequence of swing highs and
  swing lows — learning to spot these is step one of reading structure.
- **Trend vs. range**: a trend is a series of higher highs/higher lows (up)
  or lower highs/lower lows (down). A range is price bouncing between a
  ceiling and a floor with no clear directional bias.
- **Timeframes**: the same pair can look like an uptrend on the daily chart
  and a downtrend on the 5-minute chart — structure only makes sense once
  you fix a timeframe.

## Common beginner mistakes
- Treating every small wiggle as a "signal" instead of looking at the
  bigger structure first.
- Not fixing a timeframe before analyzing — jumping between timeframes
  until they see what they want to see.
- Assuming price "has to" reverse just because it hit a round number or an
  old high/low, without any other confirmation.

## Example to walk through
Show a simple uptrend: price making a higher high, pulling back to a
higher low (not breaking the prior low), then pushing to a new higher
high. Point out where support formed and why it held.

## What a student must demonstrate to pass this level
Given a plain chart (no indicators), the student should be able to
correctly label the last 3-4 swing highs and lows, and state whether the
market is trending up, trending down, or ranging — and explain why.
