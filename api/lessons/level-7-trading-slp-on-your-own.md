# Level 7 — Trading SLP On Your Own

## What this level covers
The final level: full independence. This level focuses on risk management —
the piece that protects a trader even when a well-confirmed setup doesn't work —
and pulls the entire SLP framework together into one checklist.

## Why risk management still matters
Even a well-confirmed SLP setup does not always work. Price can sweep the order
block before eventually moving in the intended direction. No amount of
confirmation (BOS, FVG, inducement, bias, engineered liquidity) makes a setup
100% certain — risk management is what protects a trader through the setups
that don't play out.

## The three risk rules
- Buy a smaller lot size — don't oversize a position because a setup looks
  strong. Confidence in a setup is not a reason to risk more than planned.
- Risk reasonably — never risk more than can be comfortably afforded to lose
  on a single setup.
- Place the stop-loss far below (or above, for a bearish setup) the order
  block — giving the zone room to be swept without stopping the trade out
  before the real move happens. A stop placed too tight, right at the edge
  of the zone, gets taken out by normal market noise even when the overall
  read was correct.

## Putting it all together: the full SLP checklist
1. Order block identified: last opposing candle before an impulsive move.
2. That move causes a confirmed Break of Structure (required, not optional).
3. FVG present in the impulsive move (raises probability to ~70% — not
   required, but strong confirmation).
4. Zone marked correctly (whole candle if body is longer than wick, body
   only if wick is longer than body).
5. Inducement present — a pullback close to the zone that traps early
   entries.
6. Rejection inside the zone confirms the entry.
7. Setup correlates with higher-timeframe market bias.
8. (Optional extra confidence) Engineered liquidity swept just outside the
   zone.
9. Stop-loss placed with room below/above the zone; position sized
   responsibly.

## Common mistakes at this level
- Skipping the risk management step because a setup passed every other
  checklist item — confirmation and risk management are separate, and both
  matter every time.
- Moving a stop-loss closer after entering because the trade "feels" safe.
- Treating one great result as proof the system doesn't need the checklist
  next time.

## What a student must demonstrate to pass this level
Given a chart, run the full 9-point checklist independently from start to
finish, including proposing a specific stop-loss placement and explaining
the reasoning for the position size relative to the setup's risk.
