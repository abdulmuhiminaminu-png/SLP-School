# Level 3 — Candles With Intent

## What this level covers
Price action patterns worth knowing — and just as importantly, which
popular ones are mostly noise.

## The core idea
A candle isn't just a shape — it's a record of who won the fight between
buyers and sellers during that period. The patterns worth learning are the
ones that reveal *rejection* or *momentum* clearly. Most "candlestick
pattern" content online teaches 30+ shapes; a working trader really only
needs a handful, understood deeply, in context.

## Key concepts to teach
- **Pin bars / rejection candles**: a long wick with a small body shows
  price was pushed one way and firmly rejected — meaningful at key levels,
  meaningless in the middle of nowhere.
- **Engulfing candles**: a candle that fully "engulfs" the prior candle's
  range, showing a shift in momentum — strongest when it appears at a
  known support/resistance level, not in isolation.
- **Inside bars**: a candle that stays entirely within the prior candle's
  range, showing consolidation/indecision — often precedes a breakout.
- **Context over shape**: the same candle shape means something completely
  different at a key support level vs. randomly in the middle of a range.
  Teach students to always ask "where is this happening?" before "what
  shape is this?"

## Common beginner mistakes
- Memorizing shapes without checking location — a "bullish engulfing" in
  the middle of a range means far less than one at a proven support level.
- Trading a pattern the instant it forms, without waiting for the candle
  to actually close.
- Ignoring volume/momentum context and treating candlestick shapes as
  standalone signals.

## Example to walk through
Show a pin bar forming exactly at a previously identified support level
(from Level 1/2), with a clear rejection wick, followed by upward
continuation. Contrast it with a similar-looking wick that formed in the
middle of a range and led nowhere.

## What a student must demonstrate to pass this level
Given a chart with 2-3 candlestick patterns marked, identify which ones
are meaningful given their location and which are just noise — and
explain the reasoning either way.
