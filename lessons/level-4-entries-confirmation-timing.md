## What this level covers
Turning everything from Levels 1-3 (structure, trend shifts, price action)
into an actual entry — where self-taught traders usually get stuck.

## The core idea
Knowing the trend and spotting a good-looking candle isn't enough — most
losing trades come from correct analysis but bad *timing*. This level is
about waiting for confirmation and defining precise entry, stop-loss, and
target levels instead of entering on a feeling.

## Key concepts to teach
- **Confluence**: an entry is strongest when multiple things line up at
  once — e.g. a rejection candle, at a key support level, in the direction
  of the higher-timeframe trend. One signal alone is weak; stacked signals
  are strong.
- **Confirmation vs. anticipation**: the difference between entering
  because you *think* price will do something, vs. entering because price
  has already shown you it's doing that thing (a closed candle, a
  confirmed structure break).
- **Stop-loss placement**: placing a stop based on where your idea is
  proven wrong (e.g. beyond the rejection wick), not an arbitrary distance
  or a round number.
- **Risk-to-reward**: only taking entries where the potential reward
  meaningfully outweighs the risk, so a trader can be wrong more than half
  the time and still be profitable.
- **Patience as a skill**: most bad entries come from boredom or fear of
  missing out, not bad analysis — teach students to recognize when they're
  forcing a trade.

## Common mistakes at this level
- Entering the instant a pattern appears instead of waiting for a full
  candle close and confirmation.
- Moving a stop-loss further away mid-trade instead of accepting the loss
  and moving on.
- Chasing a move that already happened instead of waiting for the next
  valid setup.

## Example to walk through
Combine a Level 2 structure shift + a Level 3 rejection candle at a key
level, show exactly where a disciplined entry, stop, and target would sit,
and calculate the risk-to-reward of that trade.

## What a student must demonstrate to pass this level
Given a chart at the point of a potential setup, mark where they'd enter,
where their stop-loss would go, where their target would be, and state the
risk-to-reward ratio — with reasoning for each.
